      About the data

      What you are looking at

      Each step is one National Interagency Coordination Center (NICC) Incident Management Situation Report, 3,942 of them from 2007 to 2025. Reports are daily in the fire season and weekly off season, so the timeline steps by report, not by day.

      Area colour is each Geographic Area's preparedness level (PL, 1 to 5) printed in that report. Reports often leave a quiet area's PL out; those areas show as "not listed", not as PL 1. Great Basin is one area here; before January 2015 its PL is the higher of East and West Basin.

      Heat places each incident listed in the report at one point, weighted by its committed personnel. A large complex shows as a single point, not its perimeter. About 1% of incident-days have no point and are not drawn. During playback, heat cools over a few reports so you can see where activity has just been.

      Personnel, crews, engines and helicopters are resources committed to the incidents listed in the report, summed by reporting area. They measure commitment, not shortage or total need.

      Large airtankers flying are federal exclusive-use large airtankers that flew that calendar day, from invoice records. The situation report stopped printing committed airtankers in May 2007, so this is activity, not commitment, and has no area breakdown.

      Callouts are made from the same data: each year's busiest report by committed personnel, and the first report each year at National PL 5.

      Area tiles above the map give each Geographic Area's PL and committed personnel in the report; select one to show that area's figures, select it again for national.

      International help (flags on the area tiles and in the panel, a line under the timeline) shows crews, personnel and aircraft from Australia, New Zealand, Canada and Mexico on US fires, as printed in the situation report's daily note (2008 on). The report prints no note for 2011 to 2014; those dates come from the NICC annual reports. Counts are as printed; "about N people" adds 20 per crew, the crew size the 2013 annual report gives for Canadian crews (an assumption for other crews). The annual reports sometimes give other totals or a later release date than the daily note; both are logged, not corrected.

      Sources

      • NICC Incident Management Situation Reports, 2007 to 2025, parsed by the author.
      • International help: situation report notes and NICC annual reports (2011 to 2014), compiled by the author.
      • Incident points: NIFC incident locations, matched to report incidents by the author.
      • Airtankers: USDA Forest Service FAM Aviation Business System public reports.
      • Geographic Area and Predictive Service Area boundaries: NIFC.
      • Shaded relief: computed by the author from Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data (Mapzen; sources include SRTM, GMTED2010, NED and ETOPO1; full attribution).
      • Built with d3 (ISC licence); symbols in the National Park Service map-symbol style, from the NPS symbol library (public domain) and Mapbox Maki (CC0), engine drawn by the author; flags from flag-icons by Panayiotis Lipiridis (MIT licence); fonts Barlow Semi Condensed, Inter and JetBrains Mono (SIL Open Font Licence).

      Caveats

      Counts are as printed in the reports; obvious report errors are logged, not corrected. This is research in progress for a master's thesis (MGSD) at the University of Canterbury, not an operational product.
